\chapter{Extended absorption catalogue}\label{m:app:extended-absorption}

The main text uses absorption--death as its single worked characteristics example.
This appendix retains the neighbouring models in a more compact form, preserving
the progression from a finite independent-particle system to an infinite-state
branching system without interrupting the framework-and-methods architecture.

\section{Absorption only}\label{m:app:absorption-only}

Let $X_t$ and $Y_t$ count particles inside and outside a compartment.  In the
absorption-only model, each of the $Y_t$ exterior particles moves inside at rate
$\alpha$.  Starting from $(X_0,Y_0)=(0,N)$, the generating function satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \pddt{G}=\alpha(x-y)\pdd{G}{y},
  \qquad G(x,y,0)=y^N.
  \label{m:eq:absorption-only-pde}
\end{equation}
Each initial particle remains outside until an exponential clock of rate $\alpha$
rings, and the particles act independently.  Hence
\begin{equation}
  G(x,y,t)=
  \left[\bigl(1-\ee^{-\alpha t}\bigr)x+\ee^{-\alpha t}y\right]^N.
  \label{m:eq:Gabsonly}
\end{equation}
Thus $X_t$ is binomial with parameters $N$ and $1-\ee^{-\alpha t}$, with
$Y_t=N-X_t$.  In this finite system, the independence argument is simpler than
solving the PDE that the same assumptions produce.

\section{Absorption--birth--death}\label{m:app:absorption-birth-death}

Now retain absorption at rate $\alpha$ for each exterior particle, and let each
interior particle give birth at rate $\lambda$ and die at rate $\mu$.  The
generating-function equation becomes
\begin{equation}
  \pddt{G}
  =(\lambda x^2-(\lambda+\mu)x+\mu)\pdd{G}{x}
   +\alpha(x-y)\pdd{G}{y},
  \qquad G(x,y,0)=y^N.
  \label{m:eq:abd-pde}
\end{equation}
An absorbed particle may now initiate an unbounded branching population, so the
finite three-state shortcut used in the worked example no longer closes.

A parameter-regular representation follows by conditioning on the absorption time.
Let $F(x,t)$ be the one-ancestor interior birth--death PGF solving
\cref{m:eq:bd-backward}.  One initial exterior particle either remains outside to
time $t$, or is absorbed and leaves an interior process for the remaining time.
Therefore
\begin{equation}
  g(x,y,t)=\ee^{-\alpha t}y
  +\alpha\ee^{-\alpha t}\int_0^t\ee^{\alpha v}F(x,v)\,\dd v,
  \qquad
  G(x,y,t)=g(x,y,t)^N.
  \label{m:eq:abd-convolution}
\end{equation}
This formula remains valid at $\lambda=\mu$ and at resonant parameter values, where
a separated special-function expression would require a limiting interpretation.
It also makes the normalisation checks immediate:
\[
  G(x,y,0)=y^N,
  \qquad G(1,1,t)=1,
  \qquad
  G(1,y,t)=\bigl[\ee^{-\alpha t}y+1-\ee^{-\alpha t}\bigr]^N.
\]
The fuller derivation expresses the characteristic integral through a Gauss
hypergeometric function and treats the associated resonance and
analytic-continuation domains.  Those details are retained in the donor material
but are not needed for the single-method introduction here.

\section{Recovering state probabilities}\label{m:app:coefficient-extraction}

For any bivariate PGF analytic at the origin,
\begin{equation}
  p_{i,j}(t)=[x^iy^j]G(x,y,t)
  =\frac{1}{i!j!}
  \left.
  \frac{\partial^{i+j}G}{\partial x^i\partial y^j}
  \right|_{x=y=0}.
  \label{m:eq:bivariate-coefficient}
\end{equation}
In particular, if
\[
  G(x,y,t)=\bigl(c(t)+f(t)x+g(t)y\bigr)^N,
\]
then
\begin{equation}
  p_{i,j}(t)=
  \frac{N!}{i!j!(N-i-j)!}
  f(t)^i g(t)^j c(t)^{N-i-j},
  \qquad i+j\leq N.
  \label{m:eq:trinomial-extraction}
\end{equation}
For absorption--death, the functions $c,f,g$ are the three coefficients inside the
parentheses in \cref{m:eq:Gabsdeath}, so the joint state law is trinomial.  By
contrast, \cref{m:eq:abd-convolution} is not multilinear in $x$ for
absorption--birth--death; its remaining univariate coefficients must be recovered
by differentiation, truncated-series convolution or Cauchy extraction.
