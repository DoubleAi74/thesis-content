\section{Closed-form searches and quadratic conjugacy}
\label{app:closed-form-search}

This appendix records the negative and structural calculations that are useful for
interpreting \(A(p)\), but are not needed for its probabilistic role in the main
text.  The numerical formulae below are approximations only.  They are retained
because they show how little a visually accurate fit says about the singular
quantity \(1/A(p)\).

\subsection{Exploratory formulae}

Polynomial and rational searches can be constrained to the known endpoints
\(A(0)=1/2\) and \(A(1/2)=0\).  One of the more economical rational candidates was
\begin{equation}
  \widehat A_1(p)
  =
  \frac{\tfrac12(1-2p)(1+2p)}
       {1+\tfrac12p-\tfrac52p^2-\tfrac65p^3+\tfrac65p^4}.
  \label{eq:Ahat1}
\end{equation}
It passes through both endpoints.  At the critical endpoint, however,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat A_1'(1/2)=-\frac{2}{0.55}\approx-3.636,
\end{equation}
whereas \cref{thm:A-near-critical} requires the left derivative \(-4\).
Matching the values but missing this slope is enough to make the relative error in
\(A\), and hence the error in \(1/A\), unacceptable near criticality.

A less restricted symbolic tree search produced many longer formulae of similar
visual quality.  Two representative outputs were
\begin{multline}
  \widehat A_2(p)
  =0.31696448624690005
  \log\!\Biggl(
  \Biggl|\cos\!\biggl(
  \left(\frac{1}{3.19801133824532}\right)^{1/4}\\
  {}+p\biggl[
  \sin\!\Bigl(
    \cos\!\bigl(|p|(0.621865696665606-p)
    \cos(\e^{\e^p})\bigr)
  \Bigr)+|p|
  \biggr]\biggr)\Biggr|
  \Biggr)
  +0.5982282661889831
  \label{eq:Ahat2}
\end{multline}
and
\begin{equation}
  \widehat A_3(p)
  =
  \frac{-0.616647881739505\,
  \e^{-0.552605335919693\,\e^p}}
  {\sin(\cos p)-|p|}
  +0.921964323679771.
  \label{eq:Ahat3}
\end{equation}
These expressions have no inferential status: they are records of a curve-fitting
exercise, not proposed identities.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.60\textwidth]{A3_hat_plot.pdf}
  \caption{The fitted curve \(\widehat A_3\) and product evaluations of \(A(p)\).
  The discrepancy is barely visible on this scale, although
  \(\widehat A_3(1/2)\approx9.1\times10^{-4}\) rather than zero and its endpoint
  slope is approximately \(-3.63\) rather than \(-4\).}
  \label{fig:Ahat3}
\end{figure}

One can force the correct critical branch by splicing:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat A_4(p)
  =
  \begin{cases}
  -0.04011483983619545\,\e^{\e^{2p}}
    +0.6079994336528085,
    &p<0.499962,\\[1mm]
  2-4p,
    &p\geq0.499962.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:Ahat4}
\end{equation}
This is a practical surrogate, not a closed form for \(A\).  If a splice is
needed, the certified product in \cref{eq:A-partial-product} is preferable on the
first branch and the proved asymptotic \cref{eq:A-near-critical} on the second.

\subsection{Conjugacy with the quadratic family}
\label{app:quadratic-conjugacy}

The logistic family \(f_r(x)=rx(1-x)\) is affinely conjugate to the quadratic
family
\begin{equation}
  P_c(z)=z^2+c.
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation}
  z=r\left(\frac12-x\right).
  \label{eq:logistic-affine-coordinate}
\end{equation}
If \(x'=f_r(x)\) and \(z'=r(1/2-x')\), direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}
  z'=z^2+c,
  \qquad
  c=c(r):=\frac{2r-r^2}{4}.
  \label{eq:logistic-quadratic-conjugacy}
\end{equation}
The subcritical branching window \(0<r<1\) therefore maps onto
\(0<c<1/4\).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.68\textwidth]{figure1_parameter_conjugacy.pdf}
  \caption{The parameter correspondence
  \(c(r)=(2r-r^2)/4\).  The subcritical window \(0<r<1\) maps to
  \(0<c<1/4\); the elementary monomial and Chebyshev parameters described below
  lie outside that window.}
  \label{fig:parameter-conjugacy}
\end{figure}

The interval \(0<c<1/4\) is the real radius of the main cardioid of the
Mandelbrot set.  Indeed, a fixed point of \(z^2+c\) with multiplier \(r\) has
coordinate \(z_\ast=r/2\), and its fixed-point equation gives
\begin{equation}
  c=z_\ast-z_\ast^2
   =\frac r2-\frac{r^2}{4}.
\end{equation}
Thus the endpoint \(r\uparrow1\) approaches the parabolic cusp \(c=1/4\).
This observation locates the parameter range; it is not used as evidence for a
closed-form claim.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figure4_mandelbrot_context.png}
  \caption{The image \(0<c<1/4\) of the subcritical branching interval within
  the quadratic parameter plane.  The exceptional parameters \(c=0\) and
  \(c=-2\) are shown for comparison.}
  \label{fig:mandelbrot-context}
\end{figure}

\subsection{The two elementary comparison cases}
\label{app:elementary-linearizers}

The exceptional quadratic maps conjugate to a monomial or Chebyshev polynomial
correspond to elementary linearisers.  They furnish useful algebraic checks, but
neither belongs to \(0<r<1\).

For \(r=2\), put \(u=1-2x\).  Then
\begin{equation}
  1-2f_2(x)=(1-2x)^2=u^2,
\end{equation}
and the normalised local lineariser is
\begin{equation}
  \psi_2(x)=-\frac12\log(1-2x).
  \label{eq:psi-r2}
\end{equation}
It satisfies
\(\psi_2(f_2(x))=2\psi_2(x)\),
\(\psi_2(0)=0\) and \(\psi_2'(0)=1\).

For \(r=4\), write \(x=\sin^2\theta\).  The double-angle identity gives
\begin{equation}
  f_4(x)=4x(1-x)=\sin^2(2\theta),
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
  \psi_4(x)=\{\arcsin(\sqrt{x})\}^2
  \label{eq:psi-r4}
\end{equation}
satisfies
\(\psi_4(f_4(x))=4\psi_4(x)\) and
\(\psi_4(x)\sim x\) at the origin.

There is a categorical distinction here.  At \(r=2\) and \(r=4\), the origin has
multiplier greater than one and is repelling; the formulae
\cref{eq:psi-r2,eq:psi-r4} are local repelling linearisers.  In the branching
window, the origin is attracting and the function in
\cref{eq:schroeder-koenigs} is the attracting Koenigs coordinate.  The elementary
formulae are therefore comparisons, not analytic continuations of \(A(p)\).

Finally, the theorem cited in \cref{sec:A-koenigs} concerns the function
\(z\mapsto\psi_r(z)\) at fixed \(r\).  It does not prove that the parameter
evaluation \(p\mapsto2\psi_{2p}(1/2)\) is non-elementary.  This logical separation
is the material correction to the stronger claim suggested by the original
closed-form search.
