\begin{abstract}
This chapter develops the generating-function methods used later to analyse small replicating populations and intracellular pathogen dynamics.  After a short common vocabulary of Markov chains, probability generating functions, branching criticality, and forward (master) equations, a binary Galton--Watson process provides an exact account of extinction, establishment from a finite cohort, critical survival and total progeny, and the subcritical limiting law conditioned on survival.  Its late-generation survival amplitude is identified with the classical Kolmogorov constant, represented both by a convergent product and by a Koenigs linearisation.  The discrete model is then compared with the continuous-time linear birth--death process, for which the corresponding conditional limit is explicit.  Finally, the same probabilistic structure is extended to bivariate absorption, death, and replication models.  Their forward equations lead to first-order partial differential equations for multivariate probability generating functions; independence and characteristics yield exact, normalised solutions and reliable coefficient formulae.
\end{abstract}
