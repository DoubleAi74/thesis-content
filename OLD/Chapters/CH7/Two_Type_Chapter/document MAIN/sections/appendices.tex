\section{From product of survival factors to the pathwise exponential}
\label{app:product-to-exponential}

Along a fixed path the catastrophe clock has instantaneous rate $\lambda(s)$. Over a short bin of length $\Delta t$ the chance of no trigger is about $1-\lambda(s)\Delta t$. Partitioning $[0,t]$ into $n$ equal bins of width $t/n$, the chance of no trigger on the whole interval is a product of $n$ such factors. In the constant-rate case the elementary limit
\[
\left(1-\frac{\lambda t}{n}\right)^{n}\longrightarrow e^{-\lambda t}
\qquad(n\to\infty)
\]
turns that product into an exponential. With a slowly varying rate the same mesh argument yields
\[
\exp\Bigl\{-\int_0^t\lambda(s)\,\dd s\Bigr\}
=\exp\Bigl\{-\int_0^t\bigl(\delta_1X_s+\delta_2Y_s\bigr)\,\dd s\Bigr\},
\]
which is the pathwise weight averaged in \cref{prop:feynman-kac}.

\section{Hypergeometric derivatives and resonant parameters}
\label{app:hypergeometric-details}

The derivative of the first basis function follows from the standard identity
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd z}{}_2F_1(A,B;C;z)
=\frac{AB}{C}{}_2F_1(A+1,B+1;C+1;z).
\]
Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:U-derivative}
U'(z)=\frac{AB}{C}
{}_2F_1(A+1,B+1;C+1;z).
\end{equation}

For the second basis function, set
\begin{equation*}
A_2=A-C+1,
\qquad
B_2=B-C+1,
\qquad
C_2=2-C.
\end{equation*}
The product rule gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:V-derivative}
\begin{aligned}
V'(z)=(-z)^{1-C}\Biggl[
\frac{1-C}{z}{}_2F_1(A_2,B_2;C_2;z)
+\frac{A_2B_2}{C_2}
{}_2F_1(A_2+1,B_2+1;C_2+1;z)
\Biggr].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:U-derivative} and \eqref{eq:V-derivative} show that the main result uses only shifted Gauss hypergeometric functions and elementary factors.

When $C\in\mathbb Z$, the pair in \cref{eq:UV-definitions} is not generally a fundamental pair because the two Frobenius exponents differ by an integer. A logarithmic second solution can be constructed in the usual way, or the physical solution can be obtained as a continuous limit from nearby noninteger $C$. The probability itself is fixed by the regular initial-value problem \cref{eq:backward-scaled}; only this convenient basis changes. In computation, direct ODE integration near resonance is a useful independent check.

\section{Equation-level recovery of Antal--Krapivsky}
\label{app:AK-recovery}
\label{sec:AK-recovery}

This appendix records the precise relation to Antal and Krapivsky \cite{AntalKrapivsky2011}. Their observable is the generating function of the terminal population counts; ours is the probability that a global catastrophe has not occurred. The branching skeleton (two types, one-way conversion, triangular backward structure) is shared (\cref{fig:ak-contrast}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/fig15_ak_contrast/fig15.pdf}
  \caption{Antal--Krapivsky terminal-population generating functions (left) versus the no-catastrophe probabilities studied here (right).
  Both use the same two-type one-way branching skeleton.
  Left: $\mathcal A(x,y,t)=\E[x^{X_t}y^{Y_t}]$ with initial data $\mathcal A(x,y,0)=x$, $\mathcal B(x,y,0)=y$, and no global absorbing catastrophe.
  Right: $S(t)=\Prob_{(1,0)}(\tau_{\!c}>t)$ and $G(t)=\Prob_{(0,1)}(\tau_{\!c}>t)$ with $S(0)=G(0)=1$ and catastrophe rate $\delta_1x+\delta_2y$.}
  \label{fig:ak-contrast}
\end{figure}

To make the equation-level map exact, set $\delta_1=\delta_2=0$ and let $\mathcal A(x,y,t)$ and $\mathcal B(x,y,t)$ denote the generating functions starting from one type-1 and one type-2 individual. Their initial data are
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal A(x,y,0)=x,
\qquad
\mathcal B(x,y,0)=y,
\end{equation*}
rather than the survival values $S(0)=G(0)=1$. The backward system is then
\begin{align*}
\partial_t\mathcal A
&=\lambda_1\mathcal A^2
-(\lambda_1+\mu_1+\nu)\mathcal A
+\mu_1+\nu\mathcal B,\\
\partial_t\mathcal B
&=\lambda_2\mathcal B^2
-(\lambda_2+\mu_2)\mathcal B+\mu_2.
\end{align*}
Under the rate map
\begin{equation*}
\alpha_i^{\mathrm{AK}}=\lambda_i,
\qquad
\beta_i^{\mathrm{AK}}=\mu_i,
\qquad
\nu^{\mathrm{AK}}=\nu,
\end{equation*}
these are exactly the triangular equations used in that paper, before its normalisation $\alpha_1^{\mathrm{AK}}=1$.

If instead the catastrophe rates vanish while our survival data are kept, \cref{eq:no-catastrophe-trivial} follows and both probabilities are identically one. Recovering the earlier governing system therefore requires the generating-function initial data $x,y$; the no-catastrophe formula alone does not yield a nontrivial terminal population distribution.
