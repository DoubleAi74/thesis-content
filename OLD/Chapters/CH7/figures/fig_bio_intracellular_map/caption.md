# Figure: intracellular mapping schematic (paper caption)

Biological reading of the two-type model, drawn for one macrophage-associated compartment.
Early or incompletely adapted type-1 bacteria $X_t$ replicate at $\lambda_1$, are lost at $\mu_1$, and convert irreversibly at rate $\nu$ into a later mammalian-adapted type-2 population $Y_t$, which replicates at $\lambda_2$ and is lost at $\mu_2$.
Either type may be associated with irreversible loss of intracellular containment at per-capita rates $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$; both channels enter the same absorbing event, and termination at $\tau_c$ ends the single-cell process.
Released bacteria and the extracellular phase (pale, right) lie outside the model.
The two states and the rate assignments are coarse-grained modelling constructs rather than claims of a resolved molecular release mechanism: the type-2 surface cue is schematic, and the two catastrophe arrows are drawn with equal weight because the ordering of $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$ is exactly what the four regimes below leave open.
Symbolic schematic; no parameter values are implied.

## Shorter variant (if space is tight)

Biological reading of the two-type model inside one host cell.
Type-1 bacteria $X_t$ replicate ($\lambda_1$), are lost ($\mu_1$), or convert irreversibly ($\nu$) into type-2 $Y_t$, which replicates ($\lambda_2$) and is lost ($\mu_2$).
Both types feed the same absorbing event at per-capita rates $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$; termination at $\tau_c$ ends the single-cell process and the extracellular phase (pale, right) lies outside it.
States and rate assignments are coarse-grained constructs, not a resolved release mechanism.
