\chapter[Type-specific catastrophe rates]{Type-specific catastrophe rates in a two-type branching process:\\
an exact hypergeometric formula}
\label{tt:ch:two-type}

\begin{chapterabstract}
\noindent
For a continuous-time two-type birth--death process, we derive the exact finite-time probability that catastrophe has not yet occurred. Type~1 changes irreversibly into type~2, and an individual of either type can trigger a global absorbing catastrophe. Because the catastrophe rate depends on both populations along the path, the probability is an occupation-time transform. Its backward equations are triangular: the type-2 equation is autonomous, and substituting its solution into the type-1 equation leaves a Riccati equation. After linearisation, this becomes the Gauss hypergeometric equation. A Wronskian-normalised form keeps the coefficient enforcing the initial condition regular. The calculation also gives the probability of avoiding catastrophe forever and covers the zero-birth and zero-catastrophe-rate boundaries. With the appropriate initial data, the zero-catastrophe case recovers the two-type branching equations of Antal and Krapivsky. As one reading, the types may describe early and adapted intracellular phenotypes of \textit{Yersinia pestis}, with catastrophe representing irreversible loss of macrophage containment. The formula is exact for any type-specific rate ordering, without an asymptotic or small-rate approximation.
\end{chapterabstract}
